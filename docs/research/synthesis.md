# Synthesis: Recommended Approach

> Auto-generated from the multi-agent research workflow (6 researchers + 6 adversarial source-checkers + 1 synthesizer). See [findings/](findings/) for per-dimension detail and [sources.md](sources.md) for the verified source list.

## Bottom line

Build a content-feature, two-tower contextual-preference-ranking model: encode each card from its attributes + oracle-text embedding (never a per-card ID), encode the pool with a permutation-invariant set encoder, and score each pack card by a context-conditioned dot product trained with a masked in-pack contrastive (InfoNCE) loss. This is the only published recipe that demonstrably generalizes to unseen cards (~55% vs ~22% chance, per Bertram, Fuernkranz & Mueller 2024), and it extends cleanly to "good not just human" picks by win-rate-weighting the imitation signal and filtering 17lands data to high-win-rate drafters. Train on 17lands per-set draft_data joined to Scryfall card features, and validate with a leave-one-set-out / temporal held-out-new-set protocol that reports unseen-card accuracy and GIH-WR pick-agreement separately from in-set accuracy.

## Recommended stack

### Card encoder
ID-FREE content encoder, the single most important choice for generalization. For each card compute: (a) structured features — CMC numeric, colors/color_identity multi-hot, type_line/subtypes categorical, power/toughness/loyalty numeric with explicit sentinels for */X, rarity ordinal, keywords multi-hot, produced_mana; (b) a frozen sentence-transformer embedding (all-mpnet/all-MiniLM class) of normalized oracle_text — this is the backbone of zero-shot understanding of new abilities. Concatenate and pass through a shared ~4-layer, ~1024-wide MLP (matching the Bertram et al. 2024 card encoder) to a card embedding. Pull all attributes from Scryfall oracle_cards bulk JSON, dedupe by oracle_id, cache embeddings keyed by oracle_id and recompute on errata. Handle multi-faced cards (MDFC/split/adventure/flip) by encoding each face and pooling, and planeswalkers via loyalty fields. Explicitly augment numeric magnitudes in oracle text (e.g. 'deal 3 damage') since sentence encoders under-weight numbers. Do NOT feed a learnable per-card ID embedding into the candidate path — that reintroduces the cold-start failure and forces per-set retraining.

### Context encoder (pool + pack)
Permutation-invariant set encoder over the already-picked pool. Start with Deep Sets mean-pooling of per-card embeddings (cheap, robust, handles pick 1 through 45, strong baseline) and upgrade to a 1-2 layer Set Transformer (SAB self-attention over the pool + PMA attention pooling) to capture synergy/anti-synergy that mean pooling cannot model. ISAB inducing points are unnecessary at MTG scale (pool <=45, pack <=15). Inject non-set context that does not break invariance by concatenating scalars to the pooled vector: pick_number, pack_number, and a CubeCobra-style fixed 'format/environment summary' vector (aggregate of set-level card stats) so the same model adapts across formats without per-format retraining. Optionally run light self-attention over the pack so candidates are scored relative to each other.

### Pick head
Context-conditioned scoring over the variable candidate set, NOT a fixed-vocabulary softmax. Compute one logit per card PRESENT in the pack via cross-attention / pointer-style scoring: logit = dot(pool_context_query, card_key), optionally refined by a small MLP on [pool_ctx; card; pool_ctx*card]. Apply a masked softmax over exactly the cards in the current pack (the Draftsim 'multiply logits by pack vector' trick, but here over feature-based embeddings). This natively handles any pack size and any contents including unseen cards, and yields a pick distribution P(card | pool, pack). argmax for the pick; keep the softmax for calibrated close-decision surfacing.

### Training objective
Contextual contrastive imitation as the base, then debias toward good picks. (1) Primary loss: masked, row-wise InfoNCE over in-pack cards (Bertram et al. 2024) — the picked card is the positive, all other pack cards are negatives in one term. This beats triplet loss while avoiding triplet mining; plain masked-softmax cross-entropy is the simpler, proven fallback. Both correspond exactly to 'one card preferred over the rest of the pack.' (2) Do NOT stop at vanilla behavior cloning — it provably caps at human quality and bakes in popularity/raredraft conformity bias. Add win-rate signal: filter/weight training examples by the drafter's event_match_wins and user_game_win_rate_bucket (advantage-weighted BC), as statisticaldrafting.com does. (3) Add an auxiliary multi-task head regressing a confounder-ADJUSTED card win rate (mtgds-style, regressing out deck composition/synergy/on-the-play/mulligans/player skill; raw GIH WR only as a weaker fallback since it favors controlling decks) and a head predicting ALSA (teaches wheelability / staying-open). These regularize the representation toward quality and openness without dominating the imitation target. Keep meta/usage features as optional, zeroable inputs and EXCLUDE them from any genuine release-day unseen-card evaluation since they leak and are unavailable for brand-new cards.

### Rationale
The verified, on-point evidence (Bertram, Fuernkranz & Mueller 2024, IEEE CoG best-paper nominee — note the research repeatedly misattributed this to 'Saxton'; the real authors are Bertram/Fuernkranz/Mueller) shows representation choice barely affects in-set accuracy but is the entire lever for unseen cards: feature/text encoders reach ~43% on a new set (trained on one set) and ~55.4% pretrained on a ~100M-decision corpus, vs ~22% chance, while one-hot IDs are structurally incapable of scoring an unseen card. The contextual-ranking framing (CPR -> contextual InfoNCE) is the largest verified accuracy jump on this task and models synergy, which is what distinguishes good drafting from independent card grading. The masked-softmax-over-the-pack head is the production-proven way to handle the variable candidate set. Win-rate weighting + adjusted-WR auxiliary supervision is the cheapest stable path beyond human-mimicry; full RL/self-play is deferred because no strong full-game MTG simulator exists (per Bertram et al. 2024), making win-rate reward modeling high-variance and out of scope for the core deliverable.

## Alternatives considered

- **Statistical-Drafting-style masked MLP over one-hot collection+pack vectors** — Use as the Phase-0 baseline and as a per-set fallback. It is trivially simple (~20 min CPU train), strong in-set (~70% agreement with top 17lands players), and exports to ONNX for in-browser use. Prefer it only when you do NOT need cross-set transfer for a given deployed set, since its fixed card-vocabulary head cannot score unseen cards and must be retrained per release.
- **LoRA-fine-tuned LLM with name-only prompts (UrzaGPT-style)** — Consider if you want to lean on pretrained semantic knowledge of card names and fast adaptation to new expansions; reached ~66% in-set (Llama-3-8B) vs GPT-4o zero-shot ~43%. Avoid as the primary system: cross-SET generalization is asserted but explicitly UNTESTED (evaluated only on NEO), serving is far costlier than a small MLP, and name-only discards mechanical text. Useful as an upper-bound probe or ensemble member, not the generalization workhorse.
- **Interpretable bilinear archetype-weight model (madrury-style)** — Use when transparency/debuggability matters more than accuracy or generalization (e.g. explaining picks to users). It explicitly models archetype commitment but was validated mainly on simulated data and cannot extrapolate to novel cards without precomputed archetype weights.
- **JueWuDraft-style policy/value network + MCTS lookahead on a deck-strength evaluator** — Layer this ON TOP later if the goal shifts from human-pick prediction to a maximally strong (non-mimicking) bot that plans across the 45-pick horizon. Keep it separate from the pick-prediction model since it optimizes win-rate, not imitation, and it does not itself solve unseen-card generalization (still needs content features) or a faithful MTG simulator.

## Data & evaluation

DATA. Primary training corpus: 17lands per-set draft_data CSVs (https://17lands-public.s3.amazonaws.com/analysis_data/draft_data/draft_data_public.<SET>.PremierDraft.csv.gz; verified schema, e.g. FDN PremierDraft = 640 cols: 12 metadata + 259 pack_card_<name> + 259 pool_<name>). Label = `pick`; features = pack_card_* (candidates) and pool_* (pool) + pick_number/pack_number. Join every card name to Scryfall oracle_cards bulk JSON (dedupe by oracle_id) for the content encoder. Use 17lands game_data + the card_ratings API (ever_drawn_win_rate=GIH WR, avg_seen=ALSA, drawn_improvement_win_rate=IWD, etc.) for win-rate labels/aux targets and evaluation ground truth. Filter/weight by event_match_wins and user_game_win_rate_bucket to bias toward strong drafters. Use the Draftsim M19 dataset (107,949 drafts, 265 cards, CC BY 4.0, 80/20 = 86,359/21,590 split) ONLY to reproduce known baselines (NNetBot 48.67%, CPR/SiameseBot up to 83.78% top-1) — it is single-set, simulator-derived, and has no win-rate labels, so it cannot test generalization or deck strength.

EVALUATION (report all axes, seen vs unseen separately). (1) Top-1 pick accuracy and mean pick distance (MTPD) vs the human pick. (2) Per-pick-position accuracy (1-45) to confirm the model learns MID-PACK synergy (the hard ~40% region) rather than only easy early/late picks — aggregate accuracy hides this. (3) GENERALIZATION TEST (the headline metric, build it yourself — no public benchmark exists): leave-one-set-out / temporal holdout. Train on older sets via content features, evaluate top-1 accuracy AND win-rate agreement on a strictly newer set whose cards were absent from training; report against the ~22-24% random-representation floor and the ~55% published unseen-card bar. Crucially, run a 'release-day' variant that ZEROES meta/usage features (they leak and are unavailable for brand-new cards). (4) GOOD-not-just-human metrics: does the bot take the highest adjusted-WR (or GIH-WR) available card; correlation of pick order with GIH WR; optionally estimated deck win rate via game_data. (5) Calibration of the pack-softmax for close decisions.

PITFALLS to control explicitly: popularity/conformity bias and raredrafting (compare against win-rate, weight by drafter skill); meta/format drift (segment by draft_time, expect card_ratings to shift over a set's life and across patches); survivorship bias (event_match_wins is the final record; high-win drafters overrepresented in late rounds); GIH WR confounds (favors controlling decks via longer games, entangles deck/archetype/player quality — hence adjusted WR); GP WR is ~50% noise from undrawn-card games. Name-join normalization for split/MDFC/alt-art (prefer oracle_id).

## Risks

- Verification flagged that the flagship unseen-card paper (arXiv:2407.05879) was misattributed to 'Saxton' throughout the research; correct authors are Bertram, Fuernkranz & Mueller. The headline 55% unseen-card and ~22% random figures are abstract-confirmed, but the finer numbers (in-set ~67-68%, Features+Meta+Image ~43% one-set, 512-d space) come from the PDF body and should be re-confirmed before quoting precisely.
- CPR's headline gains (>56% relative accuracy, >83% pick-distance reduction; 53.69%@D=2 up to 83.78%@D=256) and Draftsim per-bot numbers were NOT verifiable from abstracts in the fact-check (PDF extraction failures); treat exact figures as plausible-but-unconfirmed and the D=32/128 inflection claim as unverified.
- Unseen-card accuracy (~55%) is still far below in-distribution (~66-70%) and there is no consensus on which modality (text/numeric/image/meta) drives the gain — do not promise near-in-set performance on new sets, and budget ablation time to find the driver for YOUR data.
- Pick-matching accuracy is NOT the same as deck win-rate quality; Witten observed a larger, lower-skill dataset gave HIGHER accuracy but WORSE picks. Optimizing top-1 alone can faithfully reproduce a bad crowd consensus — this is why win-rate weighting and WR-agreement metrics are mandatory, not optional.
- Meta/usage features boost benchmark unseen-card numbers but LEAK future information and are unavailable at a new set's release; relying on them re-imports the cold-start problem. Keep them zeroable and exclude from release-day eval.
- Win-rate signal is deck-level, noisy, and confounded by player skill and archetype (survivorship), so naive advantage weighting can overfit already-strong archetypes; use confounder-adjusted WR and validate.
- Genuinely novel mechanics with no lexical analog in prior oracle text may still degrade zero-shot text embeddings — true mechanical novelty is the residual risk feature-based encoders cannot fully cover.
- Full RL/self-play to exceed humans is blocked by the absence of a strong full-game MTG simulator (per Bertram et al. 2024); the Saxe 'Mythic at 66%' result is a single anecdotal self-report and its repo is an imitation/transformer model, not actual self-play — do not treat it as proof the RL path is solved.
- CubeCobra's exact dimensions (128/32/160) rest on a non-peer-reviewed doc whose snippet mirrors the claim; treat the 'environment summary vector' as a sound design pattern, not precise spec.

## Roadmap

### Phase 0 — Baseline + data pipeline
- **Goal:** Reproduce known results and stand up the data join, to have a comparable yardstick.
- **Deliverable:** 17lands draft_data ingestion + Scryfall oracle_cards join (dedupe by oracle_id); a masked-softmax MLP over one-hot collection+pack vectors (Statistical-Drafting/Draftsim style). Reproduce NNetBot ~48.7% on Draftsim M19 and target ~70% top-player agreement on a recent 17lands set. Establish top-1, MTPD, and per-pick-position eval harness.

### Phase 1 — Content-feature card encoder (generalization core)
- **Goal:** Replace one-hot IDs with the ID-free content encoder so the model can score unseen cards.
- **Deliverable:** Card encoder = structured features + frozen sentence-transformer oracle_text embedding -> shared MLP. Plug into the masked-softmax head. Stand up the leave-one-set-out / temporal held-out-new-set benchmark and report unseen-card accuracy vs the ~22% floor and ~55% bar, including the meta-features-zeroed release-day variant.

### Phase 2 — Set/context encoder + contextual contrastive head
- **Goal:** Capture synergy and move to the variable-candidate contrastive objective for the largest accuracy gain.
- **Deliverable:** Deep Sets pool encoder -> upgrade to 1-2 layer Set Transformer (SAB+PMA); cross-attention/pointer scoring head; train with masked in-pack InfoNCE (fallback masked CE). Ablate: pool self-attention vs mean pooling, text-encoder family, numeric-magnitude augmentation. Inject pick_number/pack_number + environment-summary vector. Compare unseen-card and in-set metrics against Phase 1.

### Phase 3 — Good-not-just-human signal
- **Goal:** Debias from conformity and bias picks toward winning decks while staying in realistic distributions.
- **Deliverable:** Win-rate-weighted/filtered training (event_match_wins, user_game_win_rate_bucket); auxiliary heads predicting adjusted card WR (mtgds-style) and ALSA; conformity-bias mitigations (inverse-popularity reweighting, openness re-ranking). Add WR-agreement and estimated-deck-WR metrics; verify the bot deviates from crowd consensus toward higher-WR cards without losing legality/sanity.

### Phase 4 (optional/stretch) — Beyond imitation
- **Goal:** Pursue picks that exceed human demonstrators, if a reliable value signal becomes available.
- **Deliverable:** A deck-strength/win-rate value model over finished pools; advantage-weighted offline-RL fine-tuning (most stable first), and only then policy/value + lookahead (JueWuDraft-style) — kept as a separate model from the human-pick predictor. Gated on a usable game simulator or sufficiently rich outcome data; treat as experimental.
